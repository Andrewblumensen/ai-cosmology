# Strategy rethink after the 2025–26 AI-math results (2026-11-24)

Status: **proposed, awaiting the user's go-ahead.** Nothing here has been run yet.

## Why revisit the approach

How the recent AI + human attacks on hard math problems actually worked:

- **Navier–Stokes (Sept 2026).** OpenAI's claimed blow-up proof under smooth forcing
  (Clay statements C/D) used ~10,000 agents, ~88 h, ~130B output tokens, and was
  checked in Lean. Buckmaster & Alpöge posted Lean-verified forced blow-up proofs for
  related fluid equations the same day. Still contested: the forcing loophole, credit,
  no Clay verdict. DeepMind (2025) found unstable singularity families with PINNs.
- **Erdős problems.** ~100 moved to "solved" since Oct 2025 with AI help, mostly by
  literature search: open from obscurity, not difficulty. A few were genuinely new
  proofs (one 60-year-old problem in May 2026).
- **AlphaEvolve.** Evolutionary code search with an automatic scorer: matched the best
  known result on most of 67 problems and improved ~20% (e.g. the 11-D kissing number
  592 → 593).

**Common pattern:** humans pick a crisp problem and a line of attack. AI runs a massive
search in which *every candidate is scored by a machine* (Lean or a computed bound).
Humans judge whether the result matters.

## Honest diagnosis of our sessions 1–8

We copied the slogan ("LLM proposes, code computes") but not the engine. Each idea was
hand-proposed and hand-checked: 15 tested, 0 promising. The kills are useful, but the
hit rate is the warning sign. The feasibility study's original recommendation was the
missing piece: automated model search with a cheap likelihood in the loop. We set it
aside on 2026-11-24 ("no evaluator for now"). The math results are the best evidence
yet that this was the load-bearing part.

## Proposed changes

1. **Crisp, machine-scored problems instead of fuzzy anomalies.** Example: "find an
   expansion history that fits DESI DR2 BAO + Pantheon+ + compressed CMB at least as
   well as w0wa, stays physical, and uses fewer parameters."
2. **Re-score forgotten models (the Erdős lesson)** — ID-033. Hundreds of published
   dark-energy and H0 models were fitted only to pre-DESI data. Re-scoring a catalogue
   against DR2 is cheap, broad, and not done one-model-per-paper. It builds the scorer
   that item 3 reuses.
3. **Evolutionary expansion-history search** — ID-034. AlphaEvolve-style search over
   compact symbolic H(z)/w(z) forms, scored by our likelihoods, with physical
   constraints and an Occam penalty.
4. **Null test as the core of the method.** Run the identical search on ΛCDM mocks.
   The mock false-alarm distribution is our substitute for Lean.
5. **Interpretable output only.** Results must be readable formulas, not fitted
   networks (cf. NPR's "humanity learned nothing" critique of the Navier–Stokes proof).

## What does not change

- No big-lab compute: narrow, cheap problems only.
- Data decides; DESI's final release is the arbiter. DES-SN5YR remains held out.
- Idea batches continue, but feed the search engine rather than being the product.

## Recommended order

ID-033 first (fast value; builds the scorer), then ID-034.
